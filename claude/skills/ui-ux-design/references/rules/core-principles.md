# Core UI/UX Principles

## Priority

Correctness
> Clarity
> Task Completion
> Accessibility
> Consistency
> Efficiency
> Aesthetics
> Novelty

## One Primary Action

A screen or local task context should normally have one visually dominant primary action.

## Recognition Over Recall

Prefer visible labels, context, and discoverable controls over forcing users to remember hidden behavior.

## Progressive Disclosure

Show important and frequent information first.
Reveal advanced or rare options when needed.

## Feedback

Every meaningful action must provide feedback.

Typical lifecycle:

idle
→ hover/focus
→ pressed
→ processing
→ success/error

## Consistency

Same behavior should look and operate similarly across screens.

## Content Before Decoration

Design the information hierarchy before:
- gradients
- glows
- illustration
- animation
- decorative cards

## Avoid Card Overuse

Use a card when content is genuinely an independent surface.
Use spacing, section headings, or dividers for simple grouping.

## Explicit Destructive Actions

Use clear verbs and consequences.
Do not use ambiguous Yes/No confirmation for destructive operations.
